IBM HR Employee Attrition Analysis & Data Visualization Dashboard

📌 Project Overview

This project analyzes employee attrition using the IBM HR Analytics Employee Attrition & Performance dataset, containing 1,470 employee records.

The main goal of this project is to transform HR data into clear and informative visualizations that help identify patterns and differences in employee attrition.

The project focuses on Data Visualization, Exploratory Data Analysis, and Data Storytelling using Python.

---

🎯 Project Objectives

- Explore employee attrition patterns.
- Compare attrition across departments and job roles.
- Analyze the relationship between overtime and attrition.
- Compare employees who stayed with those who left.
- Visualize numerical and categorical variables.
- Identify patterns through statistical visualization.
- Present the findings through an executive-style dashboard.

---

📊 Data Visualization Dashboard

The project includes an executive-style dashboard containing multiple visualizations that summarize the main patterns found in the dataset.

Visualizations Included

1. Overall Attrition
   
   - Shows the distribution of employees who stayed and employees who left.

2. Attrition by Department
   
   - Compares employee attrition rates across departments.

3. Top 5 Job Roles by Attrition
   
   - Highlights the job roles with the highest observed attrition rates.

4. Overtime vs. Attrition
   
   - Compares attrition rates between employees working overtime and those who do not.

5. Age Distribution
   
   - Compares age distributions based on employee attrition status.

6. Income Distribution
   
   - Compares monthly income between employees who stayed and employees who left.

7. Job Satisfaction
   
   - Examines job satisfaction levels in relation to employee attrition.

8. Correlation Matrix
   
   - Visualizes relationships between selected numerical variables.

🔍 Key Findings

Overall Attrition

The dataset contains 1,470 employees, with an overall observed attrition rate of approximately 16.1%.

Department Attrition

The observed attrition rates differ between departments, with Sales showing a higher attrition rate than Research & Development in this dataset.

Job Roles

Sales Representatives have the highest observed attrition rate among the analyzed job roles, at approximately 39.8%.

Overtime

Employees working overtime have an observed attrition rate of approximately 30.5%, compared with 10.4% for employees who do not work overtime.

This represents an association observed in the dataset and does not establish that overtime directly causes employee attrition.

Employee Comparison

The analysis compares employees who stayed with employees who left across variables such as:

- Age
- Monthly Income
- Years at Company
- Job Satisfaction

These comparisons help highlight differences between the two groups.

📈 Data Storytelling

The visualizations are organized to move from an overall view of employee attrition toward more detailed factors:

Overall Attrition → Departments → Job Roles → Overtime → Employee Characteristics → Correlations

This structure helps communicate the main observations through a clear visual story.

---

💡 Analysis Insights

The analysis highlights several areas that can be investigated further:

- Certain job roles show higher observed attrition rates.
- Attrition rates differ between departments.
- Employees working overtime show a higher observed attrition rate.
- Employees who left differ from those who stayed in variables such as age, income, tenure, and job satisfaction.
- Correlation analysis provides additional information about relationships between numerical variables.

These findings describe patterns observed in the dataset and should not be interpreted as proof of causal relationships.
